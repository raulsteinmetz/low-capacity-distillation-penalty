Plan

Idea
A tiny network W learns to copy the encoder's embeddings from raw pixels.
The encoder is penalized when W copies it well.
Hope: the encoder stops relying on cheap features (color, texture) and learns harder ones.

What we know so far (CIFAR-10)
- JEPA with no regularizer collapses in 3 epochs, as expected.
- The penalty alone does not stop collapse. The encoder stays almost constant and adds a small amount of variation W cannot copy.
- So the penalty is not an anti-collapse method by itself. SIGReg does that job already.
- Why. Collapse is about the size and number of independent directions of the embeddings. The penalty only measures how they relate to the pixels. A scale-free penalty ignores size, a constant output is independent of everything, and per-dim penalties do not see dims that copy each other.
- Its possible role is choosing what the encoder keeps, on top of a spread term like SIGReg.

Results of step 1 (linear probe on CIFAR-10, chance 10%, untrained encoder 26.7%, one seed)
- no regularizer 10.0, collapsed
- penalty alone (R2) 15.6, near collapse
- penalty alone (HSIC, image or token) 10.3 and 10.0, collapsed
- VICReg 36.3, best
- SIGReg 30.7 (3 seeds, spread 0.6)
- SIGReg plus R2 penalty 34.5 (3 seeds, spread 0.2), 3.7 above SIGReg. The gain holds across seeds.
- SIGReg plus HSIC penalty 26.9 at the end, 35.6 at its best (epoch 25), then degrades
All methods learn little over the untrained encoder in this small setup, so gaps of a few points need more seeds.
Running now: VICReg plus R2 penalty (3 seeds) with two more VICReg seeds, and tinted CIFAR (SIGReg vs SIGReg plus penalty, 2 seeds each).

Step 1. CIFAR runs
VICReg, SIGReg, SIGReg plus penalty, and the HSIC versions of the penalty.
The key comparison is SIGReg alone vs SIGReg plus penalty.
Also one run of the penalty with a variance floor of 0.25 instead of 0.01. A high floor forces every dim to keep real spread, so the penalty then carries its own variance term.
Expected: no collapse, but worse than SIGReg since nothing stops dims from copying each other.

Step 2. Tinted CIFAR (started 2026-10-22, tint strength 0.4)
Add a random color tint to each image, unrelated to the class.
We measure how well a linear map recovers the tint from the embedding (tint_r2). An untrained encoder already gets 0.76.
Check if SIGReg alone encodes the tint and if the penalty removes it while keeping class accuracy.
Repeat with small embedding sizes (16 to 32 dims), where wasted dims cost the most.
This is cheap (hours) and tells us if the idea works at all.

Step 3. LeWorldModel, only if step 2 works
First Two-Room with no changes, to see how much the penalty hurts when the task state is itself a simple feature.
Then Two-Room or PushT with a distracting background, where the penalty could help planning.
Each run takes a few hours on the uniws GPU.

Expected outcome
No gain on plain CIFAR accuracy.
A possible gain where simple features are nuisances (tint, background distractors).
